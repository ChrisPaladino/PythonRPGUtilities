# Hero Creation Overview

1. Origin: Roll on the Origin table.
2. Attributes: Roll on the Level Determination table six times, once for each attribute.
3. Powers: Roll the number of powers, then determine each power’s type, specific power, and level.
4. Specialties: Roll the number of specialties, then choose or randomly roll them.
5. Description: Create the hero’s name, appearance, background, and identity.
6. Qualities: Choose three qualities; calculate Determination and Stamina.
7. Game Master Approval: Present the completed hero to the GM for approval.

## Phase 1: Origin

| 2d6 Roll | Origin                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| :------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|   2-4    | Trained: The hero is a highly skilled individual. Apparent powers normally come from superior training or specialized equipment. Gain **2 additional specialties**. You may trade **1 rolled power for 2 more specialties**.                                                                                                                            |
|   5-6    | Transformed: The hero was once normal but became superhuman through an outside agency, often an accident or experiment. Increase **one attribute or power by +2**, maximum level 10.                                                                                                                                                                    |
|    7     | Birthright: The hero was born with, or destined to develop, superhuman powers. Choose either **1 additional innate power** or **+2 to one rolled power level**, maximum level 10. The additional power should not be a device.                                                                                                                          |
|   8-9    | Gimmick: All powers come from devices. Increase **one mental attribute by +2**, maximum level 10.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|    10    | Artificial: The hero is a robot or other construct, such as a golem. Increase **Strength by +2** and gain **Life Support** in addition to rolled powers. Roll Life Support’s level normally, or trade one rolled power to increase Life Support to level 10.                                                                                            |
|  11-12   | Unearthly: The hero is an alien, elemental, angel, devil, deity, or other being from another world or dimension. Increase **two attributes or powers by +2** each. Alternately, roll twice on the Origin table, ignoring duplicate results and results of 11-12; gain both origins and apply their modifiers instead of the normal Unearthly modifiers. |

### Non-Random Origin

When creating a hero without random rolls, either:

- Choose an origin and apply its modifiers; or
- Skip origin modifiers entirely and define the source of the hero’s powers and identity during the background phase.

## Phase 2: Attributes

Roll once on the Level Determination Table for each of the hero’s six attributes:

1. Prowess
2. Coordination
3. Strength
4. Intellect
5. Awareness
6. Willpower

After applying origin adjustments, total all six attribute levels. If the total is less than 20, you may discard the hero and start over.

### Attribute Swap

After determining all six attributes, you may swap the levels of any two attributes once.
Example: A hero with Strength 3 and Awareness 7 may swap them, becoming Strength 7 and Awareness 3.

### Optional Rule: A La Carte Attributes

Instead of rolling attributes in order:

1. Roll six levels using the Level Determination table.
2. Assign the six results to the six attributes in any order.
3. You may still discard the hero if the total is less than 20.
4. You may not also use the Attribute Swap rule, because you already chose where every result went.

The GM decides whether this option is available.

## Phase 3: Powers

#### Number of Powers

| 2d6   | Number of Powers |
| :---: | :--------------: |
| 2-4 | 2 |
| 5-7 | 3 |
| 8-10 | 4 |
| 11-12 | 5 |

Origin interactions include:

- Trained: May trade one power for two specialties.
- Birthright: May choose one additional power instead of adding +2 to a rolled power.
- Artificial: Gains Life Support in addition to rolled powers.

For each power:

1. Roll on the Power Type Table.
2. Roll on that type’s specific power table using two distinguishable d6 results: a first d6 for the left column and a second d6 for the right column.
3. Determine the power’s level using the Level Determination Table.

After rolling powers, apply extras and limits according to the Powers chapter. Extras may replace rolled powers, or limits may be used to gain extras.

Optional Rule: Proportional Specialties

Instead of rolling the number of specialties:

```text
Number of Specialties = 6 - Number of Powers
```

Then add 2 bonus specialties if the hero has the Trained origin.

The GM decides whether this option is in use.

Phase 5: Description

After determining the hero’s abilities, create the hero’s description and background.

Consider:

• Place of origin
• Childhood
• Cultural or ethnic background
• How the hero acquired powers
• How the background creates present-day motivations and challenges
• Physique and build
• Costume
• Hair color and style
• Mannerisms
• Distinguishing physical features
• Hero name and civilian identity

A drawing or modified reference image may be used to establish the hero’s appearance.

────────

Phase 6: Qualities

Choose three qualities for the hero.

Qualities can be activated during play:

• To gain an advantage; or
• To cause trouble for the hero and earn Determination Points.

Good qualities should:

• Be useful for both advantage and trouble.
• Have positive and negative facets.
• Be broad enough to apply in many situations.
• Not be so broad or vague that they become meaningless.
• Often take the form of a short phrase, title, epithet, or statement.

Examples given in the source include qualities such as:

• Chosen by the Spirits
• Champion of the Tuatha
• Secret Sorceress of the Sixth Age
• Dark Defender
• Master of Minds
• Protect the Innocent
• Justice Must Prevail
• Ambassador of Peace
• Agent of Action
• Thirst for Knowledge
• Science Will Save Us
• Demon-Hunter
• Scourge of the Underworld
• Former Dark Assassin
• Crusading Journalist
• Principled Public Defender
• Strikes from the Shadows
• Non-Stop Talker
• Renowned Expert in Radiation
• Veteran of the Golden Age
• Billionaire Industrialist

Quality Design Questions

Use these questions when generating qualities:

1. Who are you? A title, role, epithet, reputation, or identity.
2. What drives you? A goal, ideal, mission, duty, obsession, or belief.
3. What gets you in trouble? A dangerous history, enemy, flaw, responsibility, or compulsion.
4. What do you do? A profession, style, habit, catchphrase, or signature behavior.
5. What is distinctive about you? Expertise, status, history, wealth, origin, or unusual feature.

The strongest qualities often answer more than one question and allow multiple interpretations.

Optional Rule: Qualities On-the-Fly

With GM permission:

• Some or all qualities may be left undefined when play begins.
• Qualities can be chosen during play as events establish the character.
• Until defined, the hero has fewer qualities to activate and may need temporary qualities.
• The GM may allow qualities to remain fluid for one or two issues.
• After that introductory period, qualities become fixed and normally require improvement to change or add.

Determination

Calculate Determination as follows:

```text
Determination Level = 6 - Total Number of Powers
Minimum Determination Level = 1
```

For this calculation:

• Each attribute above level 6 counts as a power.
• Extras and limits may change how much Determination a power costs.
• The hero begins with Determination Points equal to the Determination level.
• At the start of each issue, the hero renews to at least that many Determination Points.

Suggested Implementation Pseudocode

```python
power_cost = number_of_powers
power_cost += sum(1 for level in attributes.values() if level > 6)
power_cost += extra_determination_costs
power_cost -= limit_determination_reductions

determination = max(1, 6 - power_cost)
```

The precise effect of individual extras and limits must be taken from their entries in the Powers chapter.

────────

Stamina

```text
Stamina = Strength + Willpower
```

Use the hero’s final Strength and Willpower levels after all origin and creation adjustments.

────────

Phase 7: Game Master Approval

Present the completed hero to the Game Master.

The GM may:

• Approve the hero as written.
• Request adjustments or revisions so the hero fits the series.
• Ask for a complete rework, although the source notes this should normally require a good reason.

The GM should pay particular attention to the hero’s qualities, ensuring they are useful and appropriate for the series.

Optional Rule: Point-Based Hero Creation

With GM approval, a player may build a hero without random rolls.

Budget

• Start with 45 points, adjustable by the GM.

Costs

|Purchase         |Cost   |
|-----------------|------:|
|1 attribute level|1 point|
|1 power level    |1 point|
|1 specialty level|1 point|

Restrictions

• Spend at least 1 point on every attribute.
• Specialty levels are limited to 3, as usual.
• Only one attribute or power may be above level 8.
• The maximum possible level is 10.
• Apply power extras and limits normally.
• Choose three qualities.
• Calculate Determination and Stamina normally.

The source describes this as a fast, approximate construction method. Determination helps compensate for differences in the relative usefulness of powers.

Implementation Checklist

A Python generator should perform these operations in order:

```text
1. Roll or choose origin.
2. Roll six attribute levels.
3. Apply origin attribute modifiers.
4. Optionally swap two attributes, or use a-la-carte assignment.
5. Validate attribute total >= 20; permit restart if below 20.
6. Roll number of powers.
7. Apply origin power modifications.
8. For each power:
   a. Roll power type.
   b. Roll specific power.
   c. Roll level.
9. Apply extras, limits, and any traded powers.
10. Roll or calculate number of specialties.
11. Add origin bonus specialties.
12. Choose or roll specialties.
13. Record name, description, identity, and background.
14. Choose three qualities.
15. Calculate Determination.
16. Calculate Stamina.
17. Validate GM approval constraints.
```

Recommended Data Structures

```python
hero = {
    "origin": None,
    "attributes": {
        "prowess": 0,
        "coordination": 0,
        "strength": 0,
        "intellect": 0,
        "awareness": 0,
        "willpower": 0,
    },
    "powers": [],
    "specialties": [],
    "qualities": [],
    "determination": 0,
    "stamina": 0,
    "name": "",
    "civilian_identity": "",
    "description": "",
    "background": "",
}
```

A power record can be represented as:

```python
power = {
    "type": "Movement",
    "name": "Flight",
    "level": 5,
    "extras": [],
    "limits": [],
    "device": False,
}
```

Source Boundaries

This document covers the hero-creation procedure and tables in the specified core-book section. It does not reproduce the detailed rules for:

• Individual powers
• Power extras and limits
• Devices
• Specialty effects and specialty mastery levels
• Activating qualities
• Determination spending
• Improvements

Those mechanics are referenced by the creation chapter but are defined elsewhere in the core rulebook.
